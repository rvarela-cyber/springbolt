The Story

We wouldn’t sell you a product we hadn’t used ourselves.

Springbolt is a data intelligence and marketing infrastructure company. When the market for licensing operational data to AI labs opened up, we had the same questions you have: is this safe, is it legal, is the money real. So before offering it to anyone, we became the test case.

[ 01 ]

The starting point

Years of real operations across a modern stack: Slack, Google Workspace, Confluence, GitHub, HubSpot, Figma, monday.com, Stripe, and more than a dozen other platforms. Exactly the kind of everyday work product most companies never think of as an asset.

[ 02 ]

Counting, tool by tool

We went platform by platform with our AI data lab and counted what we had. More than 20 platforms counted. Nothing was copied or uploaded during this phase; we read counts and sizes off our own admin screens. Sessions ran about fifteen minutes each, worked around the team’s normal week.

[ 03 ]

The scrub, watched from the inside

This is the part we most wanted to see firsthand. Personal information stripped. Confidential material removed. Customer information never exposed. Originals retained only for processing, then deleted. We watched the protections we now describe to every company work on our own data first, anonymized and secured, before we’d put our name on this program.

[ 04 ]

The result

$500K collected in three months. A named U.S. buyer. A contract our counsel reviewed line by line. Full ownership retained, every original kept, and the business running exactly as before. The only thing that changed was the bank balance.

[ 05 ]

What we learned, so you don’t have to

  • The value concentrates in communications and documented process. Chat history, email, SOPs, and workflows carried more weight than raw file volume
  • Counting is lighter than anyone expects. No sorting, no cleanup, no prep
  • The legal review is where confidence comes from. Named buyer, limited purpose, no replication, ownership retained
  • Ordinary companies are sitting on extraordinary assets. If our everyday work product was worth $500K, yours deserves a real look
